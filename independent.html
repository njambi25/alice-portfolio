Independent Work

Two businesses I named, branded, and shipped.

Alongside product work, I've built and run two service businesses end to end: the name, the identity, the website, and the shape of the offer itself. Both are live and taking bookings. Every decision in them is one I had to make and then live with, which is a different kind of design experience than handing off a file.

Shots by the Sea

shotsbythesea.com ↗

Surf and ocean sports photography in San Diego. I chose the name, designed the wordmark and identity, built the site, and defined the three things it sells: action coverage, coastal lifestyle sessions, and gallery delivery.

Design decision

The gallery is the product, not the shoot. Clients are buying photographs that show up somewhere clean and easy to download, so delivery got the same design attention as the marketing pages.

Sable Pet Photography

sablepetphotography.com ↗

Outdoor pet photography in San Diego. Same scope as above: name, identity, site, and offer. The harder problem here was not visual. It was working out how someone decides to book at all.

Design decision

Two fixed tiers, The Coastal Mini and The Signature Session, instead of a custom quote for every booking. Pricing is an information architecture problem: a visible, comparable choice removes the back and forth that quietly loses people before they ever get to a conversation.

Why this sits in a product portfolio

Running something small end to end reaches parts of the work that project design usually doesn't.

Positioning comes before pixels

Naming a business and deciding who it is for is the first design decision, and everything downstream inherits it. Doing that from nothing is a useful counterweight to joining a product whose positioning was settled years ago.

Pricing is a design surface

How an offer is packaged, compared, and named changes whether people act on it. Structuring tiers turned out to be the same skill as structuring a complex form: reduce the decisions, make the remaining ones legible.

Shipping means seeing what happens next

These didn't end at handoff. They went live, real people used them, and the things that didn't work came back to me to fix. That feedback loop is the part of design that's hardest to get secondhand.
